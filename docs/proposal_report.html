01. Executive Summary & Project Abstract

Cardiovascular diseases and autonomic dysregulation remain the leading causes of preventable health crises globally. While clinical 12-lead Electrocardiography (ECG) represents the diagnostic gold standard, its reliance on specialized adhesive electrodes, high-impedance amplifiers, and cumbersome clinical hardware precludes continuous, ambulatory, and low-barrier consumer monitoring.

This capstone project proposes an end-to-end, four-stage Cardiac Cyber-Physical Digital Twin implemented entirely on ubiquitous smartphone hardware. The system eliminates external sensor peripherals by introducing a dual-modal mobile acquisition framework:

  • Primary Modality (Optical Contact PPG): Capturing high-SNR blood volume pulse waveforms at 60 FPS using the smartphone camera sensor illuminated by the camera LED flash.
  • Novelty Modality (Mechanical Chest SCG): Recording micro-mechanical cardiac recoil vibrations (Seismocardiography) at 100 Hz along the dorso-ventral axis using the phone's integrated 3-axis MEMS accelerometer placed flat on the sternum.

The raw optical and inertial signals are conditioned via zero-phase digital filtering, processed through an adaptive systolic peak-detection algorithm, and converted into clinically rigorous Pulse Rate Variability (PRV) metrics across time, frequency, and non-linear domains. A trained supervised machine learning classifier evaluates autonomic stress state and cardiac irregularity, which directly parameterizes a coupled nonlinear oscillator model to visually simulate how the patient's individual heart rhythm responds and destabilizes under synthetic physiological stress.

02. System Topology & Unified Software Architecture

The mobile client (Flutter / React Native) manages low-level hardware sensor APIs, while a high-throughput Python backend (FastAPI / WebSockets) handles heavy digital signal processing, machine learning inference, and numerical ODE simulations in real time.

03. Subsystem Breakdown & Theoretical Formulations

Stage 1 (Member A): Mobile Vision & Inertial Sensor Ingestion

Member A establishes the mobile sensing interface. Key technical contributions include:

  • Camera Parameter Locking: Disabling automatic white balance (AWB) and auto-exposure (AE) to prevent optical gain hunting, while forcing continuous torch illumination.
  • Green-Channel Spatial Integration: Computing the mean green intensity across the Region of Interest (ROI) for each video frame:
I_G(t) = \frac{1}{M \times N} \sum_{x=1}^{M} \sum_{y=1}^{N} \text{Pixel}_{G}(x, y, t) (Eq. 1)
  • Contact Verification Heuristic: Enforcing red-channel dominance thresholding ($I_R / I_G \ge 1.8$) to guarantee that a human finger is fully occluding the lens before streaming commences.
  • Inertial SCG Polling: Polling the 3-axis accelerometer at 100 Hz via mobile sensor APIs, extracting the sternal dorso-ventral axis ($a_z$).

Stage 2 (Member B — Your Role): Optical/Mechanical DSP & PRV Extraction

Member B Technical Ownership & Academic Scope
Member B acts as the mathematical and signal processing foundation of the thesis. You are responsible for transforming raw, noisy optical/inertial time-series into clinically validated cardiac biomarkers.

Your implementation consists of four rigorous mathematical modules:

  1. Digital Conditioning: 4th-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filter ($0.5\text{--}5.0\text{ Hz}$ for PPG, suppressing respiration wander and high-frequency sensor noise):
    |H(f)|^2 = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{f^2 - f_L f_H}{f (f_H - f_L)}\right)^{2n}} \quad (n=4, f_L=0.5, f_H=5.0\text{ Hz}) (Eq. 2)
  2. Adaptive Systolic Peak Detection: Combining first-order numerical differentiation, a 250 ms physiological blanking refractory period to prevent false dicrotic notch triggers, and dynamic moving-average thresholding.
  3. Peak-to-Peak Interval (PPI) Cleaning: Generating the tachogram series $PPI_i = t_{i} - t_{i-1}$ and applying physiological outlier rejection ($300\text{ ms} \le PPI \le 1800\text{ ms}$).
  4. Clinical PRV Feature Extraction:
    • Time-Domain: Mean Heart Rate (bpm), SDNN (overall autonomic variability), RMSSD (parasympathetic vagal tone), and pNN50.
      \text{RMSSD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} (PPI_{i+1} - PPI_{i})^2} (Eq. 3)
    • Frequency-Domain (FFT): Cubic-spline resampling of the uneven tachogram to $4.0\text{ Hz}$, computing the Power Spectral Density (PSD) via Welch's periodogram to isolate:
      • Low-Frequency (LF: 0.04–0.15 Hz): Baroreflex & sympathetic activity.
      • High-Frequency (HF: 0.15–0.40 Hz): Vagal tone & respiratory sinus arrhythmia.
      • LF/HF Ratio: Sympathovagal autonomic stress index.

Stage 3 (Member C): Dual-Target Machine Learning Risk Profiling

Member C ingests Member B's PRV feature table and executes dual-target supervised classification:

  • Target 1 (Autonomic Stress): Classifying acute sympathetic stress vs. parasympathetic rest, trained on the publicly benchmarked WESAD (Wearable Stress and Affect Detection) dataset.
  • Target 2 (Cardiac Arrhythmia Screening): Detecting pulse rhythm irregularity (Normal Sinus Rhythm vs. Atrial Fibrillation / ectopic beats) using the CapnoBase / MIMIC-III PPG benchmark.
  • Evaluation Methodology: Applying strict Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation to guarantee zero inter-patient data leakage, reporting precision, recall, and SHAP feature importance.

Stage 4 (Member D): Coupled Nonlinear Oscillator Heart Stress Twin

Member D translates Member C's static risk score into a dynamic, visual trajectory using a biophysical coupled Van der Pol nonlinear oscillator representing the Sinoatrial (SA) node pacemaker coupled to ventricular conduction:

\ddot{x} - \mu (1 - x^2)\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = c_{12}(y - x) + F_{\text{stress}}(t) (Eq. 4)

Member D maps Member C's risk probability directly into the damping parameter $\mu$, the coupling coefficient $c_{12}$, and simulated sympathetic drive $F_{\text{stress}}(t)$. When a healthy baseline is simulated, the phase-space trajectory ($\dot{x}$ vs. $x$) forms a stable, closed limit cycle. When an at-risk profile is subjected to synthetic stress, the oscillator exhibits a period-doubling bifurcation, visually demonstrating chaotic rhythm breakdown and tachycardia on the live screen.

04. Defense Strategy & Academic Justification

Defense Question 1: "Commercial apps already measure heart rate. What is your thesis contribution?"
Committee Answer: Commercial mobile apps are closed-source commercial utilities that output a single coarse number (pulse count) using black-box filters. Our thesis provides a fully transparent, open-source pipeline that extracts clinical-grade Pulse Rate Variability (PRV) spectral densities, couples it with an explainable ML autonomic risk model, and feeds that profile into an in-silico nonlinear cardiac dynamical twin that simulates stress degradation. No consumer application provides this digital-twin pipeline.
Defense Question 2: "Is optical PRV clinically interchangeable with ECG HRV?"
Committee Answer: Extensive clinical literature (e.g., Lu et al., 2009; Schäfer et al., 2013) demonstrates that under resting and quasi-stationary conditions, optical pulse-to-pulse intervals (PPI) correlate with ECG RR intervals with $R^2 \ge 0.95$, with RMSSD and HF power showing near-perfect clinical equivalence. We document and cite pulse transit time (PTT) delay variance directly in our report.
Defense Question 3: "Why include the Seismocardiography (SCG) chest accelerometer mode?"
Committee Answer: SCG measures cardiac mechanics (aortic valve opening and ejection recoil) rather than peripheral blood volume. Implementing SCG alongside PPG demonstrates multi-modal sensor fusion, proving deep computer engineering competence in handling high-rate IMU interrupts, multi-axis orientation decoupling, and low-frequency motion filtering.
