Executive Summary & Deliverables Matrix

CardioTwin addresses the primary clinical barrier in cardiac arrhythmia screening: accessibility. By transitioning away from bulky 12-lead ECGs and obsolete external microcontrollers (such as AD8232), the system captures cardiac mechanical vibrations using the smartphone's internal MEMS accelerometer placed over the sternum (Seismocardiography - SCG), reinforced by physiological energy gating and real-time stillness guidance.

Chapter 2: Literature Review & Deep Dive into 5 Peer Papers

Per advisor comments, Chapter 2 conducts an exhaustive comparative inquiry into 5 peer mobile cardiac systems, detailing what engineering concepts were adopted ("stolen") and how CardioTwin departs from them.

1. Lee et al. (2004) - Single-Channel ECG ICA

26th Annual IEEE EMBS Conference, pp. 224-226.

WHAT WE CAN STEAL: Independent component analysis and adaptive decomposition principles to separate motion baseline noise from cardiac pulses.
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT: Lee required physical wet chest electrodes; CardioTwin is 100% sensor-native using sternal MEMS SCG.

2. Quiroz-Juárez et al. (2018) - Cardiac Dynamics

IEEE Transactions on NanoBioscience 17(4):525-532.

WHAT WE CAN STEAL: Coupled nonlinear differential equations and modified Van der Pol oscillators for realistic heart rate dynamics.
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT: Quiroz ran static offline simulations in MATLAB; CardioTwin executes live on-device Runge-Kutta numerical integration with real-time biofeedback.

3. Tsamis et al. (2017) - Soft Real-Time Smartphone

12th IEEE International Symposium on Industrial Embedded Systems (SIES), pp. 1-4.

WHAT WE CAN STEAL: Producer-consumer circular ring buffer architecture and decoupled worker threads to guarantee sub-100ms real-time responsiveness.
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT: Tsamis relied on an external Bluetooth ECG shield; CardioTwin integrates zero-hardware on-device sensor fusion.

4. Chai (2013) - Mobile Phone ECG Architecture

IEEE 4th ICEIEC, pp. 148-151.

WHAT WE CAN STEAL: Automated threshold alert protocols and historical trend visualization for home cardiac monitoring.
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT: Chai used an external microcontroller and Bluetooth modem; CardioTwin leverages onboard edge machine learning directly.

5. Aliev et al. (2012) - ECG Noise Variance

IEEE ICPCI Conference, pp. 1-4.

WHAT WE CAN STEAL: Statistical variance thresholding to detect and discard corrupted signal segments before classification.
HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT: Aliev evaluated gross noise via an audio jack; CardioTwin uses adaptive RMS envelopes and spectral PRV profiling.

Chapter 3: Methodology & Implementation Architecture

Chapter 3 details the end-to-end mathematical signal pipeline, sensor acquisition protocol, and edge inference architecture.

Algorithm 1: Sternal Seismocardiography Signal Conditioning & Peak Picking

Input: Raw 3-axis accelerometer a(t) = [ax(t), ay(t), az(t)] at fs = 100-200 Hz, window W = 60s
Output: Heart rate (HR), SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50, LF/HF, Signal Quality Index (SQI)

1:  sigma_raw = std(az)                                  // Dorso-ventral energy check
2:  if sigma_raw < 0.005 or sigma_raw > 0.50 then        // Absolute physiological energy gate
        return REJECTED_UNVIABLE_ENERGY, prompt user to remain still and adjust sternal placement
3:  a_filt = filtfilt(Butterworth_Bandpass(0.5, 40.0, fs), az) // Zero-phase 4th-order
4:  E[n] = sqrt( (1/K) * sum_{m=-K/2}^{K/2} (a_filt[n+m])^2 ) with K = floor(0.20 * fs) // Moving-RMS envelope
5:  theta[n] = mu_E[n] + 0.60 * sigma_E[n]               // Adaptive sliding threshold (2.0s window)
6:  Identify candidate peaks p_k where E[p_k] > theta[p_k] and E[p_k] > E[p_k +/- 1]
7:  Enforce physiological refractory period: (p_k - p_{k-1}) / fs >= 0.35s (<= 171 bpm)
8:  Compute PPI_k = ((p_{k+1} - p_k) / fs) * 1000 ms; retain only PPI_k in [333, 1500] ms
9:  Compute Time-Domain PRV (HR, SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50) and 4.0 Hz Cubic-Spline Welch FFT (LF, HF, LF/HF)

Chapter 5: Conclusion, Obstacles & Future Roadmap

Chapter 5 recaps the achievements across all research objectives, transparently details the real-world engineering obstacles faced in sternal SCG sensing, and outlines the clinical future roadmap.

4 Key Technical Obstacles & Engineering Solutions

1. Posture Dependence & Motion Artifacts

Problem: Sternal SCG requires supine resting postures; torso movement, coughing, or locomotion creates large spikes that overwhelm micro-g cardiac vibrations.

Solution: Implemented pre-filter physiological energy ceiling gating (σraw > 0.50 m/s²) and gravitational posture verification with immediate on-screen stillness guidance.
2. Inanimate Tabletop Vibrations & MEMS Noise Floor

Problem: Consumer MEMS accelerometers placed on a stationary table exhibit thermal noise and ambient hum that can cause false peak triggers.

Solution: Enforced an absolute physiological energy floor (σmin = 0.005 m/s²) and SNR check (≥ 12.0 dB), achieving 100% rejection of non-biological tabletop recordings.
3. Physical Sternal Coupling Variability

Problem: Mechanical contact coupling varies across chest anatomy, clothing thickness, and phone placement over the xiphoid process.

Solution: Engineered a moving-RMS unipolar energy envelope paired with an adaptive sliding threshold (θ[n] = μ[n] + 0.60σ[n]) that dynamically scales to local amplitude.
4. Mobile OS Timestamp Jitter

Problem: Non-real-time mobile OS thread scheduling produces variable inter-sample intervals (Δt = 8 to 14 ms).

Solution: Integrated cubic-spline tachogram interpolation onto a strictly uniform 4.0 Hz grid prior to Welch FFT spectral decomposition.

Future Research Horizons

1. TinyML Microcontroller Deployment: Quantize XGBoost into 8-bit integer weights using TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers (Warden & Situnayake, 2019) for ultra-low power smart patch integration.
2. Multi-Sensor Inertial Gyroscope Fusion: Combine 3-axis gyro with 3-axis accelerometer for 6-DoF gyrocardiography (GCG) rotational compensation.
3. Multi-Center Hospital Trials: Partner with clinical cardiology units to validate sensitivity across elderly and pediatric cohorts.
